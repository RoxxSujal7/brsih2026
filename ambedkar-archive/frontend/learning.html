The Mahad Water Satyagraha (1927)

On March 20, 1927, Ambedkar led thousands to drink from the Chavdar Tank in Mahad — an act that transformed the fight for water into the first mass civil-rights movement in modern India. This event is the starting point for understanding his method of nonviolent confrontation.

📚 Intermediate Path

Major Works & Movements

Deepen your understanding through Ambedkar's major treatises, political battles, and the Poona Pact. Suitable for those with basic familiarity with his life.

5
Modules
01
The Buddha and His Dhamma (1956)

Ambedkar's definitive work on Buddhism. Completed days before his death, it reconstructs the Buddha's teachings around Prajna, Karuna, and Samata as a rationalist philosophy of equality.

BAWS Vol. 11 Buddhism Navayana
Read Vol. 11 →
02
Who Were the Shudras? & The Untouchables (1948–1948)

A two-volume historical investigation into the origins of the fourth Varna and the Untouchable communities — combining ancient texts, archaeology, and sociological analysis.

BAWS Vol. 7 History Varna System
Read Vol. 7 →
03
Round Table Conferences & The Poona Pact (1930–32)

Ambedkar's three appearances at the London Round Table Conferences, the Communal Award, Gandhi's fast, and the landmark Poona Pact that doubled reserved seats.

BAWS Vol. 2 & 9 Political History Representation
Read Vol. 2 →
04
Pakistan or the Partition of India (1940)

Ambedkar's dispassionate political analysis of the partition question — examining communalism, the two-nation theory, and the rights of minorities with rigorous legal and historical arguments.

BAWS Vol. 8 Political Theory Partition
Read Vol. 8 →
05
The Hindu Code Bill & Women's Rights (1947–1951)

Ambedkar's landmark gender-justice legislation as India's first Law Minister — equal inheritance, right to divorce, abolition of polygamy — and his resignation when conservative forces blocked it.

BAWS Vol. 14 Women's Rights Family Law

🔬 Advanced Path

Economic Theory & Constitutional Debates

For serious researchers. Engage with Ambedkar's doctoral economic dissertations, his constitutional philosophy, and the complete Constituent Assembly debate record.

5
Modules
01
The Problem of the Rupee (1923) — D.Sc. Dissertation, LSE

Ambedkar's macroeconomic masterpiece critiquing Keynes on gold exchange standards and the blueprint that influenced the creation of the Reserve Bank of India in 1935.

BAWS Vol. 6 Monetary Economics RBI Foundation
Read Vol. 6 →
02
Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India (1925)

Ambedkar's Ph.D. dissertation from Columbia University analyzing fiscal federalism, revenue sharing, and the economic structure of British colonial India.

BAWS Vol. 6 Fiscal Policy Columbia PhD
Read Vol. 6 →
03
Constituent Assembly Debates: Complete Record

All of Ambedkar's speeches in the Constituent Assembly — 1948–1949. His defense of Articles 14, 15, 17, 21, 32, universal adult franchise, and his famous final address on November 25, 1949.

BAWS Vol. 13 Constitutional Law Primary Source
Read Vol. 13 →
04
Philosophy of Hinduism & Buddha or Karl Marx (1987)

Ambedkar's philosophical comparison between the Buddhist Dhamma and Marxist dialectical materialism — arguing Buddhism offers a superior ethical framework for social equality.

Riddles in Hinduism (1987) — Posthumous

Ambedkar's unfinished critical examination of the Vedas, Upanishads, Epics, and Hindu philosophy — one of his most incisive scholarly works, published posthumously.
