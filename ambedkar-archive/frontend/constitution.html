The Preamble

The Soul of the Constitution

📜 Preamble to the Constitution of India (1949)

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST, SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, Social, Economic and Political;
LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

📌 Adopted by the Constituent Assembly on November 26, 1949. Source: BAWS Vol. 13 · Dr. Ambedkar as Principal Architect of the Constitution of India.
Chronological Journey

The Drafting Process: 1947–1950

🏛️
August 29, 1947
Drafting Committee Constituted

Dr. Ambedkar appointed Chairman of the seven-member Drafting Committee by the Constituent Assembly. He immediately began synthesizing 60+ constitutions and legal traditions.

📝
October–November 1947
First Constitutional Draft

Ambedkar and his team produced the first complete draft with Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles, federal structure, and an independent judiciary.

⚖️
November 4, 1948
Draft Presented to Assembly

Ambedkar introduced the Draft Constitution with a landmark speech defending Parliamentary democracy and universal adult franchise for a newly independent nation.

🗳️
1948–1949
114 Days of Clause-by-Clause Debate

7,635 amendments were tabled; 2,473 were accepted. Ambedkar personally answered nearly every objection, defending Article 32, Article 17, and the Preamble with constitutional precision.

🎤
November 25, 1949
Final Address to the Assembly

Ambedkar delivered his historic final address warning of the "contradiction" between political equality and social-economic inequality, urging India to resolve it or face democratic collapse.

📜
November 26, 1949
Constitution Adopted

The Constitution of India was formally adopted by the Constituent Assembly. November 26 is now observed as Constitution Day (Samvidhan Diwas) across India.

🇮🇳
January 26, 1950
Constitution Enacted — Republic Day

India became a Republic. The Constitution, the world's longest democratic written constitution, came into force. Ambedkar's vision of a casteless, egalitarian legal order was now fundamental law.

Fundamental Rights & Key Provisions

Articles Championed by Ambedkar

Art. 14
Equality

Right to Equality Before Law

The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within the territory of India. Ambedkar derived this from the 14th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution.

"Equality before law is not merely political — it means equality of social opportunity."
— Constituent Assembly Debates, BAWS Vol. 13

Art. 15
Non-Discrimination

Prohibition of Discrimination

The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. Ambedkar insisted this article be non-derogable.

Ambedkar added Clause 4 to permit the State to make special provisions for SCs, STs, and women — the constitutional basis for reservation.
— BAWS Vol. 13, Constituent Assembly
📖 Read in Vol. 13
Art. 17
Core Ambedkar Article

Abolition of Untouchability

"Untouchability" is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of Untouchability shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law.

"Untouchability is a crime against humanity — not merely against society. It must be abolished not just in law but in practice."
— BAWS Vol. 13, Constituent Assembly Debate on Art. 17
📖 Read in Vol. 13 Vol. 5: Untouchability
Art. 21
Liberty

Protection of Life & Personal Liberty

No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Ambedkar ensured this was a substantive, not merely procedural, right.

Ambedkar advocated for "due process" (American model) before settling on "procedure established by law" as a necessary compromise with the Assembly.
— BAWS Vol. 13
📖 Read in Vol. 13
Art. 25–28
Religion

Freedom of Religion

Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion. Ambedkar was alert to ensure this freedom could never be used to perpetuate caste-based discrimination inside religion.

Ambedkar ensured that religious freedom does not protect religiously-sanctioned social disabilities — the anti-untouchability reading.
— BAWS Vol. 13
📖 Read in Vol. 13
Art. 32
Heart & Soul

Right to Constitutional Remedies

The right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of fundamental rights. Ambedkar called this "the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it" — making rights meaningless without a remedy.

"If I was asked to name any particular article in this Constitution as the most important — an article without which this Constitution would be a nullity — I could not refer to any other article except this one. It is the very soul of the Constitution."
— BAWS Vol. 13, Constituent Assembly, December 1948

Art. 44
Directive Principle

Uniform Civil Code

The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India. Ambedkar saw this as necessary for gender justice across personal laws.

Linked to his resignation over the Hindu Code Bill — Ambedkar believed codified family law was essential for women's equal rights.
— BAWS Vol. 14: The Hindu Code Bill
📖 Hindu Code Bill (Vol. 14)
Art. 326
Universal Franchise

Universal Adult Suffrage

Elections to Lok Sabha and all State Assemblies shall be on the basis of adult suffrage — every citizen 21+ years (later 18+) has an equal vote regardless of caste, religion, or literacy.

Ambedkar strongly opposed any literacy or property qualifications for voting — he insisted universal suffrage was the only democratic foundation for a newly free nation.
— BAWS Vol. 13
